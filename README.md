1. Introduction: A Common Reference Within an Already Unified System
Scientific disciplines commonly use energy units suited to their characteristic scales. Particle physics frequently employs electron-volts, chemistry uses molar energy quantities, and engineering commonly reports joules or kilowatt-hours.

These conventions do not represent disconnected physical systems. The SI already provides a coherent framework, and the relevant quantities can be converted using established definitions and constants. The motivation for an additional reference is therefore practical rather than foundational: a shared numerical baseline may make selected comparisons easier to display, discuss, and reproduce.

The present proposal introduces FQCU as such a baseline within the Tanfarid Quantum Thermodynamic Universe programme.

Its intended role is limited:

FQCU expresses specified energy quantities as dimensionless multiples of a fixed terahertz photon energy while preserving the original quantity, conditions, and uncertainty.

A common energy unit does not make different physical processes equivalent. Chemical free energy, particle rest energy, translational kinetic energy, and emitted radiation remain distinct physical quantities even when expressed in the same unit.
